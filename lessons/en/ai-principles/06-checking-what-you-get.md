# Checking what you get

AI principles · Chapter 6 of 8 · 14 minutes

A polished answer can hide a wrong figure, a made-up source or a missing fact, and a model may agree with you just because you said it, so you check what comes back and refine until it passes.

## Goals

- Find the four errors planted in a polished report, and say which kind each one is.
- Choose the follow-up that fixes the most, and see what a second check finds.
- Pass the check.

## The read

### Finished is not the same as right

Chapter 5 was about how to ask. This one is about what comes back. An answer can look finished, with a title, headings and a number in a box, and still be wrong. Judging it means checking three things.

Facts: the names, numbers, dates and sources in it. Check the ones that matter against something you trust, such as your own notes, and work out key numbers yourself. Reasoning: whether each conclusion follows from the facts given. Gaps: what is left out that would change the conclusion.

### Polish lowers our guard

That finished look works against you: people tend to check finished-looking answers less. As chapter 2 showed, smooth words show what was likely, not what is true. A model can even write a source that does not exist, such as a study with a believable name: chapter 3 called this confabulation. If you cannot open a source and find the claim in it, treat the claim as unconfirmed.

### It may just agree with you

Models tend to agree with the person asking, even when that person is wrong. This is called sycophancy. A likely reason is training: models learn partly from people’s ratings of their answers, and people tend to rate agreement well. So if your question holds a guess, such as “this is why sales rose, right?”, the answer may hand your guess back to you as a fact. Ask it to test the guess against the facts instead.

### Describe, check, refine

The habit that helps is a loop. Describe what you want, as in chapter 5. Check what comes back. Then refine: name each problem and ask again. Vague feedback such as “make it better” tends to change the wording, not the facts. Check the revision too, because a new version can fix one error and add another. Use the result only once it passes.

## Check

Put the steps of working with AI on a task in order.

1. Describe what you want, with the facts only you know.
2. Check the answer’s facts, reasoning and gaps.
3. Name each problem and ask for a revision.
4. Check the revision the same way.
5. Use the result once it passes your check.

**Why:** Describe, check and refine is a loop, and a revision is a new answer: it can fix one error and add another, so it gets the same check as the first one. You use the result only once it passes.

## What you can do now

- Check an AI answer’s facts, reasoning and gaps against a source you trust.
- Spot a made-up source, a wrong figure and missing context, even in a polished report.
- Notice when an answer only repeats your guess, and ask it to test the guess against the facts.
- Write a follow-up that names each problem, and check the revision before you use it.
