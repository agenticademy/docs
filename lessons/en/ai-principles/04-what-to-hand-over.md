# What to hand over

AI principles · Chapter 4 of 8 · 14 minutes

You can do a task yourself, work on it together with AI, let AI do it, or set up an agent; what is at stake, how easily you can check the result and whether you want to learn the skill decide which.

## Goals

- Place five tasks on the delegation board.
- Change the stakes, how easy a task is to check and “I want to learn this”, and read the reason for the advice.
- Pass the check.

## The read

### Four ways to work

Chapter 3 showed that AI can do one task well and fail at the next, sometimes in a confident tone. So decide first how much of a task to hand over. There are four ways.

Do it yourself: you do the work, and AI stays out of it.

Augment, which means to add to what you can do: you lead and swap ideas and drafts with AI, and you make the decisions. Planning a trip with an AI chat open beside you works like this.

Automate: you hand AI one clear job, it hands back a result, and you check that result before you use it. Turning a set of recipes into a shopping list is an example.

Set up an agent: an agent is a program that uses a model to take steps for you, such as reading emails and filing them. You give it a goal and limits once. After that it does the work each time it comes up, and you look in now and then, not at every step. It suits work that keeps coming back, like sorting a café's inbox every morning.

### Three questions

What is at stake if it goes wrong? A missing item on a shopping list costs little. A careless reply to a customer who fell ill after a meal can cost a lot.

How easy is it to check the result? You can check a shopping list against the recipes in a minute. Reading a trip plan does not tell you whether its opening times and prices are right.

Do you want to learn or keep the skill? If AI writes the summary of a chapter you are revising, you skip the work that makes you remember it.

A lot at stake and a result you cannot check point to doing it yourself, or with someone who can check it. Little at stake and a quick check point to handing it over, and to an agent if the task comes back every day. Wanting to learn points back to you.

### A rule of thumb

In the visual you place five tasks on a board, then move the three questions for each task and watch the advice change. The advice comes from a short scripted rule. You may know something about a task that the rule does not, so there is no single right answer.

## Check

### Question 1 of 3

Every morning a café gets about forty emails: orders, complaints and questions. A wrong label is easy to spot at a glance and costs little. Which way fits best?

- Do it yourself: sorting email is no job for AI.
- Work through each email together with AI, back and forth.
- **✓** Set up an agent that sorts them each morning, and look at its work now and then.
- Hand it to AI once and never look at the result again.

**Why:** The task comes back every day, little is at stake and a glance shows a wrong label, so an agent fits, with a look now and then. Doing it yourself works, but spends your time where little is at stake. Working through each email together is slow for a simple sort. Never looking again drops the check that catches mistakes.

### Question 2 of 3

You are revising for a history test and want to remember the material. Which use of AI keeps the learning?

- **✓** Write the summary yourself, then ask AI what you missed.
- Ask AI to write the summary of each chapter, then read it.
- Set up an agent that writes a summary of every chapter for you.

**Why:** Writing the summary is the practice that makes you remember. Asking for feedback afterwards keeps that practice and still catches gaps. A summary written for you, by AI or by an agent, skips the work the test is about.

### Question 3 of 3

On the board, the advice for a task moves from “Automate” to “Do it yourself”. Which change could do that?

- The task now comes back every day.
- You no longer want to learn the skill.
- **✓** A mistake would now cost a lot, and you could not easily spot one.

**Why:** A lot at stake and a result you cannot check call for a person in charge. A task that comes back every day moves the other way, towards an agent. Not wanting to learn the skill also points towards handing it over, not back to you.

## What you can do now

- Name four ways to work on a task: do it yourself, augment, automate or set up an agent.
- Use what is at stake, how easy the result is to check and whether you want to learn the skill to choose one.
- Explain why the same task can need a different way of working when one of those changes.
