# AI principles

Beginner · No code · 8 chapters · 111 min

For anyone who uses AI tools or wants to. You train a tiny model in the page, watch another one write word by word, and practise deciding what to hand over, how to ask, and how to check what comes back. At the end you can explain how these systems work and use them with judgement.

## Before you start

Nothing.

## By the end you can

- Explain in plain words what an AI model is and how it produces text.
- Say where AI tends to do well and where it fails, and why a confident answer can still be wrong.
- Decide what to hand over to AI, and describe what you want so it can help.
- Check what you get back, keep private data out, and say when AI helped.
- Name who sets the rules for AI, and choose how much an agent may do on its own.

## Chapters

1. [What AI is, and what it is not](01-what-ai-is.md): Most AI systems in use today learn patterns from examples and use them to infer an output for something new. Such a system is not a mind, a database or a search engine, and its examples shape every answer.
2. [How a model writes](02-how-a-model-writes.md): A language model writes one token at a time by drawing from a list of chances, temperature changes how it draws, and nothing in that step checks whether the words are true.
3. [Strong here, weak there](03-strong-here-weak-there.md): AI does some hard tasks well and fails at easy-looking ones, can state false things with confidence, and is more useful when it says it does not know.
4. [What to hand over](04-what-to-hand-over.md): You can do a task yourself, work on it together with AI, let AI do it, or set up an agent; what is at stake, how easily you can check the result and whether you want to learn the skill decide which.
5. [Saying what you want](05-saying-what-you-want.md): Where a request leaves a gap, the model often fills it with a likely guess, so a good request describes the result, the way to work and the behaviour you want, with the facts and an example only you can give.
6. [Checking what you get](06-checking-what-you-get.md): A polished answer can hide a wrong figure, a made-up source or a missing fact, and a model may agree with you just because you said it, so you check what comes back and refine until it passes.
7. [Privacy, bias and owning the result](07-privacy-bias-ownership.md): What you paste into an AI tool may be kept, so share only what the task needs; bias comes from data and design and falls on some people more than others; say when AI helped, and own what you send.
8. [Who sets the rules](08-who-sets-the-rules.md): Shared principles, company principles, written constitutions and laws shape how AI is built and used, and you choose how much an agent may do on its own.
