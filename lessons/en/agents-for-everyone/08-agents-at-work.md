# Using agents well at work

Agents for everyone · Chapter 8 of 8 · 14 minutes

Choosing what to hand to an agent, briefing it clearly, and checking what it did.

## Goals

- Place six work tasks on the risk grid.
- Build a brief with all four parts and see the result improve.
- Pass the check.

## The read

### What to hand over

Ask two questions about any task. How bad is it if the result is wrong? And how easy is it to check? A meeting summary is easy to read through, and a mistake costs little: hand it over. A change to a supplier's bank details is hard to check and can send money to the wrong place: keep it.

Most tasks sit in between. For each one, choose a level: the agent does it, the agent asks you first (the approval from chapter 7), or you do it yourself.

### Brief it like a new colleague

An agent knows only what you tell it, so a good brief has four parts. The goal: what you want done. The context: the facts it needs, such as names, amounts and dates. The limits: what it must not do. And "done when": how you will know the work is finished and right. Leave one out and the agent fills the gap with a guess.

### Check the work, and stay responsible

Read what the agent produced before it goes out, at least until you trust it on that kind of task, and spot-check it after that. An agent acts on someone's behalf. That person, or their organisation, stays responsible for what it does.

### Trying agents yourself

You do not need a paid plan to try one. Open models are models whose makers publish them so anyone can run them, such as NVIDIA's Nemotron family. Hugging Face, a website where makers share their models, has many more. As of November 2026, two services let you use many of them for free, with limits on how much: OpenRouter's free models, and NVIDIA's developer catalogue (build.nvidia.com), which is meant for trying models out. A small open model can also run in your own browser. This academy's developer path, “Build your first agents”, shows how to connect to OpenRouter and to a model in your browser.

## Check

### Question 1 of 3

Which task should you keep for yourself, or at least approve every time?

- Summarising a meeting.
- **✓** Changing a supplier's bank details.
- Tidying a shared folder.

**Why:** A wrong bank detail sends money to the wrong place, and it is hard to spot. High harm and hard to check means a person decides. A summary and a tidy folder are easy to check and cheap to fix.

### Question 2 of 3

What are the four parts of a good brief?

- **✓** The goal, the context, the limits, and how you will know it is done.
- A greeting, the task, a deadline and a thank-you.
- The tool names, the model name, the price and the time.

**Why:** An agent knows only what you tell it. Context and limits prevent wrong guesses, and "done when" lets you check the work.

### Question 3 of 3

An agent sends a wrong reply to a customer for you. Who is responsible?

- The agent.
- The company that made the model.
- **✓** You, or the person who set it to act for you.

**Why:** An agent acts on someone's behalf. That person, or their organisation, stays accountable, which is why checking the work matters.

## What you can do now

- Decide whether a task should be done by an agent, approved by you or kept by you.
- Write a four-part brief for an agent.
- Explain why a person stays accountable for an agent's work.
